RNU Pipeline

Systematic discovery of pathogenic spliceosomal small nuclear RNA (snRNA) variants in pediatric rare disease cohorts.

Background

Most genomic diagnostic pipelines focus primarily on protein-coding genes, leaving many noncoding regions of the genome unexplored. Among these overlooked regions are spliceosomal small nuclear RNA (snRNA) genes, which play essential roles in pre-mRNA splicing and have recently emerged as an important class of rare disease genes.

To address this gap, I developed a pipeline to systematically identify, annotate, and prioritize variants in disease-associated snRNA genes from whole genome sequencing data.

The Challenge

More than half of individuals with suspected rare genetic disorders remain without a molecular diagnosis. One reason is that standard analysis pipelines often prioritize protein-coding variation and may completely overlook pathogenic variants located within noncoding spliceosomal RNA genes.

Although variants in genes such as RNU4ATAC, RNU6ATAC, RNU12, RNU4-2, and RNU2-2 are now known causes of Mendelian disease, these loci are frequently absent from routine variant prioritization workflows.

RNU Pipeline

Our RNU Pipeline was designed to systematically interrogate noncoding spliceosomal RNA genes across prospective and retrospective genomic datasets.

  • Interrogates known disease-associated snRNA loci.
  • Annotates variants with gnomAD population frequencies.
  • Incorporates ClinVar classifications.
  • Integrates previously reported pathogenic variants from the published literature.
  • Supports large-scale reanalysis of existing genome sequencing cohorts.

By focusing specifically on spliceosomal RNA genes, this RNU pipeline enables identification of variants that are often missed by conventional clinical and research analysis workflows.

Cohort Findings

  • 16 affected individuals identified.
  • 12 unrelated families.
  • Diagnoses across both major and minor spliceosome disorders.
  • Novel and previously reported pathogenic variants.
  • Prospective discovery and retrospective reanalysis cases.

Identified pathogenic variants included disease-causing alleles in RNU2-2, RNU4-2, RNU4ATAC, RNU6ATAC, and RNU12.

Transcriptomic Validation

To investigate the biological impact of identified variants, we performed RNA sequencing on affected individuals, family members, and control samples.

Patients carrying pathogenic variants in minor spliceosome genes demonstrated widespread retention of U12-type introns, a molecular signature consistent with impaired minor spliceosome function.

We identified significant intron retention across dozens of genes, many of which are known U12-intron-containing genes. These findings provide transcriptomic evidence linking pathogenic snRNA variants directly to abnormal splicing activity.

Impact

  • Identified diagnostic variants in 16 affected individuals.
  • Expanded the known mutational spectrum of multiple snRNA disorders.
  • Demonstrated the value of systematic noncoding variant analysis.
  • Linked pathogenic variants to transcriptome-wide splicing defects.
  • Established a reusable framework for interrogating spliceosomal RNA genes in rare disease cohorts.
