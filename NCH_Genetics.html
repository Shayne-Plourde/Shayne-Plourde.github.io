Institute for Genomic Medicine, Nationwide Children’s Hospital

Next-generation sequencing technologies have fundamentally changed the study of human genetics. Since the draft human genome was completed in 2001, thousands of disease-associated genes have been catalogued. Genetic testing has become a key diagnostic tool, especially for developmental disorders and rare genetic diseases.

Despite these advances, our understanding of genotype–phenotype relationships remains incomplete. Clinical genetic testing yields a molecular diagnosis in only 25–30% of referred patients, underscoring the need for deeper insight into the biological impact of thousands of genetic variants.

The incredible throughput and diverse applications of high-throughput sequencing instruments promise transformative discoveries. However, they also present significant bioinformatics challenges. The BIT team develops and deploys scalable computational tools to process raw genomic data, call and annotate variants, and prioritize candidates for further investigation.

Selected Projects

Structural Variant Pipeline

Clinical-grade pipeline for structural variant detection and interpretation from whole genome sequencing data.

WGS Structural Variants Rare Disease

  • Integrates GRIDSS and Manta structural variant callers to improve sensitivity.
  • Annotates genes, OMIM disease associations, and regulatory regions.
  • Incorporates cohort-specific recurrent variant databases to filter common events.
  • Reduces candidate variants from >10,000 calls to <30 prioritized variants per case.
  • Supports cohort-scale rare disease reanalysis and gene discovery.

Presented at: American Society of Human Genetics (ASHG), 2025

Impact: Transformed structural variant interpretation from a largely manual process into a scalable cohort-level workflow. By reducing more than 10,000 raw SV calls to fewer than 30 high-priority candidates per case, the pipeline reduced review burden by approximately 90% and helped identify candidate disease-causing variants in previously unsolved patients.

RNU Variant Discovery Pipeline

Framework for systematic discovery and interpretation of pathogenic spliceosomal small nuclear RNA (snRNA) variants in rare disease cohorts.

snRNA Rare Disease RNA-seq Noncoding Variants

  • Interrogates disease-associated spliceosomal RNA genes frequently overlooked by conventional pipelines.
  • Integrates ClinVar, gnomAD, and published literature to prioritize candidate variants.
  • Aggregates cohort-level genomic data to identify recurrent and rare disease-associated alleles.
  • Supports both prospective analysis and reanalysis of whole genome sequencing datasets.
  • Incorporates transcriptomic validation through RNA sequencing.

Results: Identified diagnostic variants in 16 affected individuals from 12 unrelated families with pathogenic variation in RNU2-2, RNU4-2, RNU4ATAC, RNU6ATAC, and RNU12.

Impact: Expanded the genetic and transcriptomic landscape of spliceosomal RNA disorders while demonstrating the value of systematic noncoding variant analysis in rare disease diagnostics.

PacBio Long-Read Genome Sequencing Pipeline (pbGS)

Clinical and research workflow for analysis of PacBio HiFi sequencing data, enabling comprehensive detection of structural variants and challenging genomic variation missed by conventional short-read sequencing.

PacBio HiFi Long-Read Sequencing Structural Variation Rare Disease

  • Processes PacBio HiFi whole genome sequencing data using a standardized analysis workflow.
  • Supports detection of structural variants, repeat expansions, and complex genomic rearrangements.
  • Participated in development and validation efforts for clinical implementation of long-read sequencing.
  • Integrates seamlessly with existing genomic analysis infrastructure.
  • Provides improved resolution in regions that are difficult to analyze using short-read technologies.

Impact: Helped establish long-read whole genome sequencing as a complementary diagnostic technology, expanding the ability to detect clinically relevant genomic variation in previously challenging cases.
