Structural Variant Analysis Pipeline

I designed and implemented a structural variant analysis pipeline that transformed SV interpretation from a largely manual process into a scalable workflow capable of supporting cohort-scale rare disease studies.

Background

Structural variants (SVs), including deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations, and complex rearrangements, represent an important source of genetic variation but remain difficult to analyze at scale. While whole-genome sequencing has improved rare disease diagnosis, many analysis pipelines focus primarily on single nucleotide variants (SNVs), small insertions/deletions (indels), and copy number variants (CNVs). As a result, potentially pathogenic structural variation often remains unexplored in unsolved cases.

To address this challenge, I designed and implemented a structural variant analysis pipeline for rare disease research at the Institute for Genomic Medicine. The goal was to enable systematic interrogation of structural variation within a cohort of patients who remained without a molecular diagnosis after conventional genome analysis.

The Challenge

Structural variant callers such as GRIDSS and Manta are highly sensitive but typically produce more than 10,000 candidate structural variants per genome. Reviewing this volume of data manually is impractical and creates a major barrier to incorporating SV analysis into routine rare disease workflows.

We sought to develop a scalable filtering framework capable of reducing thousands of calls to a manageable set of high-priority candidates for analyst review while maintaining sensitivity for disease-causing events.

Pipeline Design

The pipeline combines structural variant calling, functional annotation, recurrent-event detection, and population-based filtering to prioritize clinically relevant variants.

  1. Structural variants are detected using multiple SV callers, including GRIDSS and Manta.
  2. Candidate variants are annotated with overlapping genes, transcript information, and known OMIM disease associations.
  3. Intergenic and low-priority variants are removed to dramatically reduce analytical burden.
  4. Internal recurrent structural variant databases are generated from unaffected individuals enrolled in the same research study.
  5. Common recurrent events are flagged and excluded, allowing prioritization of rare potentially pathogenic variants.

This strategy reduces the candidate set from more than 10,000 structural variants per genome to fewer than 30 variants requiring detailed review.

Cohort Analysis

The pipeline was applied to a cohort of unresolved rare disease cases enrolled through our translational research program.

  • ~300 families enrolled in the rare disease study.
  • ~70% remained unsolved after initial genomic analyses.
  • First 90 unsolved cases evaluated with the SV workflow.
  • Candidate structural variants identified in 7 individuals.
  • Included single-exon deletions and complex genomic rearrangements.

These findings demonstrate that structural variation represents an important source of previously overlooked pathogenic variation in rare disease cohorts and can substantially improve diagnostic yield when analyzed systematically.

Impact

Prior to development of this workflow, structural variant analysis was rarely performed because analysts were required to manually review raw structural variant call sets containing more than 10,000 events per genome. The volume of candidate variants made comprehensive review impractical for most cases.

By combining gene annotation, disease-gene prioritization, and recurrent-event filtering, the pipeline reduced the number of candidate variants requiring manual review to fewer than 30 per case. This reduced analyst review time by approximately 90% and enabled structural variant analysis to become a routine component of rare disease investigations.

  • Reduced candidate variants from >10,000 to <30 per case.
  • Reduced analyst review burden by approximately 90%.
  • Enabled systematic structural variant analysis across large cohorts.
  • Identified candidate pathogenic variants in previously unsolved patients.
  • Presented at ASHG 2025.
